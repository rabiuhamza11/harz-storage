Share Advertisement A memorandum of understanding (MoU) has been signed for a $3.5 billion petrochemicals project in Bonny, Rivers State, between the project promoters, a Chinese engineering company and a local landowner. The proposed project will occupy 500 hectares of land owned by Sene Kalada, a Wari Alabo (house chief), in Bonny. The MoU was signed in Port Harcourt by Frank Okafor, representing Best Option Chemical Limited, Lee Edwards of CNCEC, and Kalada. Chamberlain Peterside, director-general of the Rivers State Investment Promotion Agency (RSIPA), facilitated and witnessed the agreement. CNCEC is expected to provide 80 percent of the project financing, with the balance to come from other partners through land contributions and other inputs, according to the project promoters. Best Option Chemical Limited, described as a subsidiary of Best Option Petro-Meto in Spain, will coordinate engagement with regulatory authorities, including the Nigerian National Petroleum Company (NNPC), and host communities, while CNCEC will serve as the financial partner, Okafor said. The plant is expected to produce methanol and other petrochemical products for the domestic market and export. The project, which may witness physical construction in less than nine month according to the promoters, is expected to produce methanol and other petrochemical products that would be consumed locally and for export. The proposed investment is expected to generate jobs during construction and operations, potentially adding to Rivers State’s petrochemicals industry, which includes Indorama’s facilities in Eleme. On why Bonny was selected over Ahoada and Onne, the promoters said its access to gas, a key feedstock for the proposed plant was responsible for the decisoin. Bonny hosts major oil and gas operations, including Nigeria LNG, which processes natural gas for export. The investors said the concentration of hydrocarbon companies in the area made it attractive for the project. Okafor also cited the planned road link to Bonny as a factor in the decision, saying improved road access would reduce dependence on water transport. Peterside said RSIPA had facilitated discussions involving Onne, Ahoada and Bonny before the investors settled on the island. He attributed the investment interest to improved political stability and confidence in the state’s security environment. He said the agency would continue to support the project and ensure transparency as the promoters work towards further agreements. Peterside described the MoU as an initial step in the investment process, with additional agreements expected as the project advances.
