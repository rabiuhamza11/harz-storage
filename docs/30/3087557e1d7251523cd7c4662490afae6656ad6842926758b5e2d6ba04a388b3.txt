Sexual violence concepts launched online are spilling into the real world – harming women and young men and overwhelming counselors Published: October 16, 2026 8:23am EDT The online forums and sites that make up the manosphere host a common thread: They mobilize sexual violence against women. Tate is a prominent figure [in the manosphere](https://www.unwomen.org/en/articles/explainer/what-is-the-manosphere-and-why-should-we-care), a loosely connected network of online forums and sites united by beliefs in male supremacy. This messaging has increasingly moved to mainstream platforms, as social media algorithms on sites like YouTube and TikTok steer young men and boys toward manosphere content sometimes [within minutes](https://www.dcu.ie/antibullyingcentre/recommending-toxicity) of opening an account. Tate’s behaviors are an extreme example of manosphere ideologies spilling into the physical world, [harming women as well as the men and boys](https://www.anrows.org.au/resources/guide-to-the-manosphere/) taken in by the messaging. The growth of the manosphere has been [attributed to](https://doi.org/10.1080/14680777.2022.2029925) [backlash toward the #MeToo Movement](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11199-024-01526-6), which encouraged women to speak up about sexual violence. There is also evidence that increased isolation and time spent online during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://theconversation.com/youd-better-start-paying-attention-to-the-manosphere-youre-living-in-it-279547) contributed to many men finding these communities. [Nearly half of U.S. women](https://www.cdc.gov/nisvs/media/pdfs/sexualviolence-brief.pdf) experience unwanted sexual contact in their lifetimes, and [1 in 5 experience attempted rape or rape](https://www.cdc.gov/nisvs/media/pdfs/sexualviolence-brief.pdf). Some countries are taking initiative to curb sexually violent content online. For instance, the U.K. in 2026 implemented several guidelines for social media companies to [reduce the spread](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/tech-companies-must-go-above-and-beyond-to-protect-women-and-girls-from-online-abuse-or-face-further-action) of misogynistic and sexually violent content [to young men and boys](https://theconversation.com/dont-blame-toxic-masculinity-for-online-misogyny-the-manosphere-is-hurting-men-too-254802). But U.S. federal and state legislation is not keeping up. As [social scientists](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=tDyjU-oAAAAJ&hl=en) in [social work](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=YNQCrTMAAAAJ&hl=en) [and psychology](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=tSrJRyIAAAAJ&hl=en), we study the ways sexual violence and masculinities intersect with digital technologies. [The growth](https://fortune.com/2026/08/15/manosphere-multibillion-dollar-industry-adin-ross-looksmaxxing-report/) and [cultural salience](https://theconversation.com/youd-better-start-paying-attention-to-the-manosphere-youre-living-in-it-279547) of manosphere communities have made them increasingly relevant to the work we do. A [violence prevention specialist](https://menengage.org/resources/10-ways-to-engage-men-and-boys-to-counter-backlash) we talked to in October 2026 said he is struggling to engage men and boys and to keep up with a changing landscape that [includes online messaging](https://theconversation.com/we-analysed-the-tiktok-history-of-142-men-heres-what-it-taught-us-about-the-manosphere-282156). For every Andrew Tate there are [many more people launching misinformation](https://www.worcester.ac.uk/about/news/academic-blog/health-and-wellbeing-blogs/under-the-influence.aspx) and hateful content about women through [topics that seem less directly tied to it](https://doi.org/10.1177/17416590251351446), like working out, playing video games or just talking. These conversations connect with peoples’ day-to-day lives, which makes it easy to then slip in disparaging comments about women. Masculinity educator Ben Hurst said he’s been inundated with requests to talk about the rise in online misogyny influencer culture. People who counsel sexual violence survivors are also struggling with the added burden of online influence, said Kim Foster, a police department senior victim assistance specialist we also interviewed in October. The women Foster works with are often “expecting to be belittled,” she said. “That’s what they’re seeing online.” The network Our team of experts [reviewed 105 studies](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224499.2026.2670470) on manosphere communities, including research on nine contemporary social media sites: Reddit, Twitter/X, Twitch, Facebook, TikTok, 4chan, Telegram, Instagram and YouTube. We found that they collectively hosted 89 unique manosphere communities, not counting YouTube channels. Seventy-five of those communities were on Reddit. Our studies also included data from 195 independent websites and blogs. These communities use discussions of sexual violence to [help recruit and indoctrinate](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224499.2026.2670470) new members. They are united by the idea that feminism has gone too far and women’s rights should be rolled back. Male supremacists assert that men are [owed sexual access to women](https://doi.org/10.1177/0959353519857754) and frame themselves as victims when women exercise their right to [decline sex or a relationship](https://doi.org/10.1080/14680777.2025.2512233), or seek to [hold men accountable](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11199-024-01526-6) for sexual harm. This content is not confined to niche communities. A 2024 survey of 16- to 24-year-olds in the U.K. found that 95% were [familiar with Tate](https://hopenothate.org.uk/2024/07/24/plugged-in-tate-misogyny-2/), and 41% of boys and men had a favorable view of him. Finding belonging In the context of [rising rates of loneliness](https://www.apa.org/pubs/reports/stress-in-america/2025/index.html#methodology) in the U.S., a [shared badge of victimhood](https://doi.org/10.1177/13675494251371661) helps recruit and engage men into manosphere communities, where they can find [belonging and shared purpose](https://doi.org/doi:10.7282/t3-44as-5910) as they cultivate antagonism between genders. A. et al](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/701155) Discrediting survivors Men of the manosphere use what researchers call the [DARVO strategy](https://dynamic.uoregon.edu/jjf/articles/freyd97r.pdf): deny, attack and reverse victim and offender – to frame #MeToo and anti-sexual violence activism as a [feminist conspiracy targeting men](https://doi.org/10.1558/jld.25747). “I’d rather be raped than falsely accused,” [a Men’s Rights forum user](https://commons.lib.jmu.edu/diss202029/39/) posted in 2018, showing one way that social media users in the manosphere [argue that men, not women](https://doi.org/10.5204/ijcjsd.v5i2.310), are the true victims of sexual violence. This positions women as a [shared enemy](https://doi.org/10.1177/1097184X17706401) for men to blame for [loneliness, frustration or anger](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11245-023-09921-6). Manosphere members claim that false rape allegations [happen all the time](https://doi.org/10.1177/09593535211033461) and point to [skewed statistics](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11199-024-01526-6) and cherry-picked stories. In reality, research consistently reports that a small minority – [roughly 5%](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-015-0666-2) – of sexual assault allegations investigated by police are proven to be false. Manosphere communities also judge what forms of sexual violence are worthy of taking seriously. Online forum quote used as an example in a scholarly paper by Yi Ting Chua et al. [Chua, Y. T. et al.](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10610-023-09555-9) A common trope: [“real victims” stay silent](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11199-024-01526-6). Online forum quote used as an example in a scholarly paper by Heather Hensman Kettrey et al. [Kettrey, H. H. et al](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11199-024-01526-6) Foster sees this affecting her work: “I think we had really hoped that after the #MeToo Movement, people would be more confident coming forward, but I don’t feel like victims expect to be believed and taken seriously,” she told us. The #MeToo Movement encouraged women to speak up about sexual violence, but was met with an online backlash that has silenced some women. [Gareth Fuller/PA Images via Getty Images](https://www.gettyimages.com/detail/news-photo/rape-victim-is-interviewed-by-a-police-officer-in-an-news-photo/832726278) Defending their worldview offline Manosphere communities contain [many references to violence and rape](https://doi.org/10.17615/w7sa-qs12). Participants often call these protected speech or [innocent jokes](https://doi.org/10.1080/01639625.2022.2142864). But for some men, [they are a bridge](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224499.2026.2670470) [to further harm toward women](https://doi.org/10.1177/00104140241312095). Pick-up artist and seduction communities [teach members](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcm.2020.100402) that “no” rarely actually means “no.” They teach that women deny sex as a game or to avoid being seen as a slut, a concept which they call “last minute resistance.” Users share detailed stories of sexual encounters – referred to as [field reports](https://ijoc.org/index.php/ijoc/article/view/20283) – to give advice or ask about overcoming last-minute resistance. Our research found that these stories often represent [clear disclosures](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224499.2026.2670470) of men sexually assaulting women. [Wright, D.](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dcm.2020.100402) The manosphere socializes boys and men into a worldview where entitlement, victimhood and male supremacy are normalized – making it more difficult for them to achieve [healthy, consent-based, gender-equitable relationships](https://doi.org/10.1080/09540253.2024.2315052). [Laskovtsov, A.](https://encompass.eku.edu/etd/662/) This isn’t happening organically As social media algorithms [push more manosphere content](https://www.isdglobal.org/publication/algorithms-as-a-weapon-against-women-how-youtube-lures-boys-and-young-men-into-the-manosphere/), these types of beliefs surface in the real world. In a case involving a male teacher preying on a female student, for instance, the investigation found notes on tactics from a pick-up artist forum in the teacher’s possession, Foster said. Australian researchers found in 2026 that [40% of teenage boys say they think women lie](https://theconversation.com/40-of-teenage-boys-believe-women-lie-about-domestic-and-sexual-violence-new-research-276978) about domestic violence or sexual assault. The [U.K.’s Online Safety Act](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/online-safety-act-explainer/online-safety-act-explainer) calls algorithms that promote or normalize hatred and sexual violence against women an [online safety problem](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/ofcom-tech-firms-must-up-their-game-to-tackle-online-harms-against-women-and-girls). The act advises tech companies to [remove creator payments](https://digiday.com/marketing/how-every-major-platform-pays-creators/) for misogynistic posts and diversify algorithmic content types to lessen the impact of [digital echo chambers](https://www.esafety.gov.au/young-people/echo-chambers-and-your-feed). But U.S. federal policy on social media does not call out the algorithmic spread of misogynistic content as a concern. The proposed [Kids Online Safety Act](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1748/text) calls for online platforms to prevent and mitigate harmful material, such as physical violence or sexual exploitation of children, but [does not mention women](https://www.congress.gov/bill/119th-congress/senate-bill/1748/text#id44feef84-d084-43a7-8595-cbdedf4d2e0e). State legislation proposed in California in 2026 would have fined large social media companies for algorithms spreading hate speech or violent content, [including promotion of violence against women](https://legiscan.com/CA/text/SB771/2025). But the bill was [vetoed by Gov. Gavin Newsom](https://www.politico.com/news/2025/10/13/newsom-siding-with-tech-giants-vetoes-online-hate-speech-bill-00606600). New York’s [Stop Hiding Hate Act](https://ag.ny.gov/press-release/2025/attorney-general-james-announces-social-media-companies-must-start-reporting), enacted in 2025, requires large social media companies to report their content moderation policies and how they respond to hate speech. But [the law](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/bills/2023/S895/amendment/B) doesn’t address algorithm transparency or gender-related hate speech. Worsening trends Negative societal beliefs about sexual assault victims have long contributed to low rates of reporting, prosecution and jail time for perpetrators. About [1 in 3 survivors](https://rainn.org/facts-statistics-the-scope-of-the-problem/statistics-the-criminal-justice-system/) [report sexual assault](https://bjs.ojp.gov/library/publications/criminal-victimization-2024) to law enforcement. Meanwhile, a low percentage – [just a few percent](https://www.nbcnews.com/specials/sex-assault-convictions/) – of perpetrators [serve any prison time](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2018/10/06/less-than-percent-rapes-lead-felony-convictions-least-percent-victims-face-emotional-physical-consequences/). Based on our research, we believe that manosphere messaging about sexual violence worsens these trends and allows those who wish to cause harm to act without feeling the threat of punishment. Until changes are made to the algorithms that spread this type of content on social media, these problems are likely to continue to grow. “I don’t know how to prevent this from happening without widespread systemic change,” Foster told us.
