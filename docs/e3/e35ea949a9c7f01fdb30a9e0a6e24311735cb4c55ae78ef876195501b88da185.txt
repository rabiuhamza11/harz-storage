Olayemi Cardoso, CBN governor, said the apex bank was looking to strengthen collaboration with Chinese financial institutions, particularly in payment systems, cross-border transactions and knowledge-sharing, while exploring opportunities to attract greater investment from Asian markets into Nigerian financial instruments. Speaking Saturday at the Nigerian delegation’s closing press briefing at the 2026 International Monetary Fund and World Bank Group Annual Meetings in Bangkok, Thailand, Cardoso said the planned engagements in China would move beyond high-level discussions to identify practical areas of cooperation. The planned China visit follows Cardoso’s recent engagements in Singapore, which formed part of Nigeria’s broader effort to deepen financial and economic ties with Asian markets, and reflects the CBN’s push to strengthen institutional cooperation, explore opportunities for cross-border financial collaboration and attract more Asian investment into Nigerian markets. “We are basically interested in seeing how we can deepen collaboration between institutions, Chinese banks, Nigeria Central Bank, and others, and the payment system in particular,” he said. China’s payment infrastructure offers potential lessons for Nigeria, Cardoso said, adding that the CBN was interested in peer reviews and exchanges between the two countries’ institutions. He disclosed that the initial discussions had taken place at leadership level during the Annual meetings, with more detailed engagements expected during the planned visit to China. “So the first engagement we had here was an engagement at the top. And then once we go to China, people will be rolling up their sleeves and coming down to brass tacks as to what makes sense and what doesn’t,” Cardoso said. The CBN governor also pointed to the potential for Chinese and other Asian investors to increase their exposure to Nigerian financial instruments, noting that investment from the region had remained limited despite the substantial liquidity available in its markets. He said Nigeria’s improved macroeconomic stability could help change that pattern by reducing the currency risks that previously discouraged long-term investment. “The chances of getting long-term Naira capital have increased astronomically as a result of the stability that we are finding in markets,” Cardoso said. He cited the narrowing gap between Nigeria’s official and parallel foreign-exchange rates as evidence of the improvement, saying the premium, which had previously reached about 60 percent, had declined to around 3 to 4 percent. The reduction in exchange-rate distortions, he said, had eased a major obstacle to financial institutions seeking to develop long-term naira-denominated financing products. Cardoso also highlighted the banking sector’s recapitalisation exercise as part of efforts to strengthen Nigeria’s financial system and improve its capacity to support economic growth in an exercise which raised N4.65 trillion, with just over 70 percent of the funds coming from domestic investors. Cardoso said the CBN’s responsibility was to maintain monetary and price stability, while development finance institutions should channel capital into productive sectors of the economy. He identified the Africa Finance Corporation (AFC), which he now chairs, the Bank of Industry (BOI) and the Development Bank of Nigeria (DBN) as institutions expected to play a greater role in mobilising financing as market conditions improve. The governor said the CBN would work more closely with the AFC to strengthen its capacity to mobilise resources in Nigeria and across Africa. Cardoso also stressed the importance of policy consistency and transparency in sustaining investor confidence, saying international investors closely monitor economic developments and policymakers’ decisions. His comments come as Nigeria seeks to convert recent improvements in macroeconomic indicators into stronger investment and economic growth. Headline inflation fell to 14.63 per cent in September 2026, while external reserves have risen above $55 billion, according to figures Cardoso cited in earlier remarks. The CBN governor said consolidating those gains would require sustained policy discipline, stronger institutions and closer cooperation with international partners. He expressed optimism that stronger financial ties with China and other Asian economies could open up new channels for investment, trade financing and payment-system cooperation.
