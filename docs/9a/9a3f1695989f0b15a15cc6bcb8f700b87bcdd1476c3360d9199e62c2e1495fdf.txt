The USMNT’s kids are thriving, and ‘they have a bit of swag to them’The post-World Cup youth movement has paid off with historic plays that, for the moment, feel natural, sustainable and neededJeff Rueter in St Paul, MinnesotaMon 5 Oct 2026 08.00 EDTLast modified on Mon 5 Oct 2026 13.45 EDTSharePrefer the Guardian on GoogleFrom the outside, a moment like the one Cavan Sullivan produced Saturday seemed to be pulled directly from dreamland. Turns out, it pretty much was.“I was amazed. I was excited,” the 17-year-old said of learning he’d start for the US men’s national team against Mexico, in what would become a 3-0 win. “I texted my family right after, and after lunch, I took a nap. Before I go to sleep, I visualize and try to manifest good things for the night. Some things come to fruition, some don’t.”Sullivan visualized scoring, and celebrating with a knee slide towards the corner. Hours later, he was doing exactly that, picking off a poor pass from goalkeeper Raúl Rangel and chipping him from 35 yards out to become the USMNT’s youngest-ever goal scorer, sliding toward the corner flag after a full sprint with his teammates in hot pursuit.“That wasn’t the way I pictured scoring a goal, but the celebration was,” Sullivan said.The more incredible thing about Sullivan’s moment, aside from its historic nature and its semi-dreamy origins, is how normal it is for this window for the US. All but one of the 13 potential debutants called in by Mauricio Pochettino have seen time on the field, most of them impressing. Julian Hall, 18, has scored in all three of his appearances. Mathis Albert broke Christian Pulisic’s decade-old benchmark to become the program’s youngest goalscorer and held the record for a whole four days before Sullivan’s chip.View image in fullscreenMathis Albert was briefly the youngest goal scorer in USMNT history. Photograph: Taylor Banner/USSF/Getty ImagesGiven the opponents so far, it’s fair to say these players aren’t inching into the shallow end of the pool. They’ve been thrown in at the deep end, and they are swimming.Peru and Chile missed this summer’s World Cup, but they are far from pushovers. While the US struggled to keep them off the scoreboard, the Americans made up for defensive miscues with a commitment to attacking, scoring four goals in each friendly.Cavan Sullivan’s stunner makes him youngest-ever USMNT scorer in romp over MexicoRead moreThe back half of this long-winded window was primed to be even more enlightening, with Saturday’s win over Mexico and Tuesday’s date with Canada still to come. Newly appointed captain Tyler Adams has said that these games will help determine the region’s top dog. Their final opponent of the quartet will hardly give them an inch, with Canada raring to take another step in Jesse Marsch’s second cycle at the helm.On Saturday, the US showed they can weather a storm. Pochettino’s young side were unable to get anything going in the attacking half for most of the first 45 minutes. Mexico stymied the hosts with a man-marking scheme that was appreciated by the El Tri faithful, whose numbers in the stadium dwarfed those of the US. And yet, as Pochettino put it, a lineup with an average age of 23 years and 25 days “suffered together” without conceding before a shock US opener flipped the script.Young? Bet, as they’d say.“It’s really important to play this type of game,” Pochettino said. “To build our experience, to know what [is needed] to play this type of game, and the adversity. I think it will help when we start the qualification for the World Cup. I think that experience will help the team to manage the pressure and stress in a different way. Sullivan has quickly found ways to affect the game at right wing, a role Pochettino largely entrusted to Sergiño Dest throughout the World Cup summer. Hall’s exceptional off-ball movement makes him another capable goalscorer as an alternative to Folarin Balogun (and even more competition for Ricardo Pepi).Others also look like safe bets to stick around. Albert and Justin Ellis are dynamic options for the team in transition. Neil Pierre and Adri Mehmeti could be useful additions in central defense and midfield, respectively. There’s little room for complacency among Pochettino’s core.“They’re battling for it, and I love to see it,” center-back Chris Richards said Saturday. “I mean, the young guys, they’re hungry. They have a bit of swag to them, and that’s something that I’ve been very impressed with.”View image in fullscreenAdri Mehmeti and Julian Hall have each impressed in their first international camp. Photograph: Bill Barrett/ISI Photos/Getty ImagesThis mass debut event differs considerably from the last one, which immediately followed the US’s failure to qualify for the 2018 World Cup. That period saw the emergence of Adams, Weston McKennie, Antonee Robinson and Tim Weah, giving the fanbase sorely needed fresh faces. They joined a team with few, if any, senior figures around for the rebuild.This time around, the young guys can get their sea legs without the additional pressure.“I think the balance that we’ve been able to have in lineups, surrounding them with guys that have experience, they can go under the radar for some parts of the game,” Adams said. “They don’t have to put in crazy, crazy performances. We just want to get them in the good spots where they’re comfortable to score goals, to go 1v1, to show the qualities that they have, and we were able to do that.”The trick, then, will be to keep the good vibes of this camp from being an aberration – to avoid the wild fluctuations that have defined much of Pochettino’s USMNT tenure so far.“We need to protect them. We need to support them,” Pochettino said. “Our responsibility now is [ensuring] that this group of talented players [will] grow in the right direction. Today, they were so excited to play. Again in St Louis, against Chile, they were so excited, and they were so excited in playing against Peru in Orlando.“We need to continue with that idea, keep that feeling. Every time that they can be part of this national team is the most important. OK, maybe it’s tough to say that, but I think that is what we expect [as] a coaching staff. It’s not ‘play well or not’. It’s that commitment and the desire to play, that today is the best and the most important game in your life. That is what we try to translate.
