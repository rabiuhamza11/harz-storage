Key takeaways Fainting is a temporary loss of consciousness due to your brain not getting enough oxygen. While usually not serious, talk with a doctor if you have repeated fainting to rule out underlying medical conditions. Various factors can trigger fainting, including emotional stress, pain, dehydration, and certain medications. Health conditions can also trigger fainting, such as diabetes, heart disease, or anxiety. To assist someone who has fainted, raise their feet to support blood flow to their brain and loosen any restrictive clothing. Make sure they remain lying down or seated for at least 10 to 15 minutes. Seek immediate medical care if the person does not regain consciousness quickly or has any concerning symptoms.
