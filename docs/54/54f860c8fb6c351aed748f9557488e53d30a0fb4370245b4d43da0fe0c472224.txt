and tell Nigerians this is the direction, and everybody will queue,” Lukman said.The G-100 was set up to encourage opposition parties to rally behind one presidential candidate who could challenge the ruling APC in 2027.The move followed an all-opposition summit held in Ibadan in April, where participants agreed to pursue a consensus candidate.According to Lukman, the process has expanded beyond the six political parties that initially signed on, with efforts underway to bring more parties into the alliance.He explained that political parties and their presidential candidates had taken over the process from the G-100 and were now negotiating directly to build the trust needed to reach an agreement.“When the parties owned the process we had started and took it beyond, took it out of our hands and began to engage each other based on the need to develop the kind of confidence they need to trust one another,” Lukman said.He said the lack of trust among opposition parties and their presidential candidates remained one of the biggest obstacles to forming a united front ahead of the 2027 election.Lukman said a summit held on August 31 agreed to establish five working groups to handle different aspects of the proposed alliance, including the selection of a single presidential candidate.According to him, each participating party would nominate members to the relevant working
