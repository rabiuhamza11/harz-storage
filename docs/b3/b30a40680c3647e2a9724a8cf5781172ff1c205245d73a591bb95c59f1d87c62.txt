How one library system was transformed on a tight budgetThis article is more than 14 years oldAnythink Libraries in Denver, Colarado, have quadrupled circulation and visitor numbers in seven years by connecting with users and raising its own levyPam Sandlian SmithWed 2 Nov 2011 09.02 EDTSharePrefer the Guardian on GoogleAdams County libraries in Colorado have risen from the ashes, thanks to community support for funding and a dramatic rethink of management and strategy. The libraries in Adams County were historically the worst-funded in the state. They were old-fashioned, small and unappealing. But a systematic overhaul has resulted in a system that is inspiring others to rethink their services.In the US, many libraries are moving toward the special taxing district form of governance. Within this structure, which is overseen by an appointed board of directors, there is a dedicated levy from property taxes that ensures a stable level of funding. In the Denver metropolitan area, the Rangeview Library district operates in this way and became independent from Adams County in January 2004. It now operates as a special library-taxing district and runs the Anythink libraries, comprising seven branches and one bookmobile that provide services to more than 320,000 people.Anythink is now receiving awards and honours: visitor numbers and circulation has quadrupled. The community is proud of its four bright new buildings and three renovated libraries. Customer satisfaction is at an all-time high.Anythink's library philosophy has undergone complete transformation over the past four years. An Online Computer Library Center research study – From Awareness to Funding – demonstrated that people who have transformational experiences in libraries consistently vote for increased funding for libraries. The "experience model" Anythink developed based on this study underpins the new philosophy.From the moment the customer enters the library, they feel inspired and at home. The library is designed as a space to discover information organically. To foster this, the district is working on experience zones where a customer might sit on the floor and build geodesic domes with newspaper, learn how to raise bees or become a gardener – interactive experiences stumbled upon throughout the library.Anythink had to ensure that its libraries weren't only providers of databases and books but also places for people to connect with ideas in a different way. Two key models of inspiration for this reinvention are London's Idea Stores and the Apple store. All Anythink's new libraries are designed for comfort, intuitive browsing and self-service. This allows staff to focus on hospitality, gracious service and creating connections with people, information and programmes.The district's seven libraries now offer 100,000 square feet of space – up from 35,000. They are a book-lover's idea of heaven, full of beautiful, gorgeous books. In addition, Anythink is moving toward greater use of digital offerings and looking at ways to connect reluctant users to library services.The district actively pursues additional funding and grants and is now being invited to partner on new grant initiatives. Anythink recently applied for funding to connect teens with media so that the organisation can develop projects to support production of music and video.Like many libraries, Anythink is moving toward providing downloadable books, movies and music and continues to look for products to support staff, such as handheld devices that allow them to help users find items, check them out and email them an acknowledgement of return dates. Digital developments are not viewed as a threat to the library's longevity but as an opportunity to take a step forward and work with new technology.Traditional skills are also catered for. The district provides programmes such as pie making and knitting. Psychologist and philosopher Jean Piaget notes that, for children, their work is play; that is how they discover and learn about the world. Anythink is designed around the idea that adults and teens need to be reintroduced to this discovery method of learning. The goal is to make learning fun and playful for all ages.Anythink's transformation was achieved on a very tight budget that tripled in recent years, but the district remains the second-worst-funded library system in Colorado. However, what makes a tremendous difference is that the funding is predictable and dedicated. The library board is receptive to new ideas and shares the organisation's ethos of making the library as user-friendly and customer-centric as possible. Staffing was restructured to help make the dollars go further, and output has quadrupled within this leaner structure.Anythink's smaller team of 110 full-time equivalent staff covering seven branches is customer-focused, passionate about its work and proves its brilliance daily.To everyone's great surprise, Michelle Obama awarded Anythink Libraries the 2010 National Medal for Museum and Library Service last December at the White House. No one could have imagined such an event just a few short years ago when there was no money even to buy paper or pencils.
