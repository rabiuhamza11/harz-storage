Gears 5 review – thrills, kills and belly laughs in a refreshing rebootThis article is more than 7 years oldPC; Xbox OneA female protagonist, comedy one-liners and increased tactical options bring subtlety without sacrificing adrenalinRick LaneFri 6 Sep 2019 02.00 EDTLast modified on Wed 6 Sep 2023 10.38 EDTSharePrefer the Guardian on GoogleThe Gears of War series has made me laugh many times over the years, but Gears 5 may be the first time it’s done so on purpose. Developed by Microsoft subsidiary the Coalition, the latest sequel surrounds the familiar cover-shooting mechanics with upgrade systems, side-missions and even large open spaces to explore. But perhaps Gears 5’s most significant addition is a sense of levity.Far from the grey, hyper-serious tone of the original trilogy, Gears 5 brings us a wide-ranging adventure that is often as gaudy as it is gory. The campaign’s introductory chapter sees players exploring an abandoned missile silo hidden on a tropical island, a location choice that seems more befitting of Uncharted than Gears. The script, meanwhile, is laced with daft one-liners and pithy retorts, embracing the series’ inherent absurdities rather than bulldozing through them.From the off, Gears 5 clearly wants players to have fun. The story centres around Kait, promoted from sidekick in Gears 4 to the series’ first female protagonist (Marcus Fenix assumes a supporting role for the second game in a row). After the Locust were resurrected in the previous title, Kait has begun seeing strange visions of the swarm, triggering a personal quest that sees Gears depart from many of its long-established conventions.View image in fullscreenDeparts from convention … Gears 5. Photograph: MicrosoftAfter a first act that reintroduces the basics, Gears 5 suddenly ditches its typically linear levels, offering several vast environments for the player to explore. The first of these is a stunning arctic landscape where the ice crackles beneath Kait’s feet and a towering supercell peppers the ground with razor-sharp hail. You traverse this glittering expanse using a sailing skiff, gliding nimbly across the ice and snow as you search for landmarks to investigate.Structurally, Gears 5 bears similarities to Metro Exodus, using its open-ended maps to enhance the sense of adventure rather than offering true freedom. Each map has only a handful of side-activities, all of which revolve around combat. Yet whether you’re plundering an abandoned mine or helping a group of desert nomads get their water pumps running, scouring the fringes of Gears 5 is rarely less than entertaining.It helps that the combat still, quite literally, rips. Six games down the line, introducing a Locust to your chainsaw bayonet is yet to get old, and the game’s familiar cover and sprint mechanics retain their satisfying sense of weight. Fans of the series will be pleased to hear there are more ways to make things bleed than ever, including a version of the iconic Lancer rifle that fires cluster-grenades, and the ludicrously powerful Breaker Mace, which lets Kait punt Locust halfway across the map like fleshy golf-balls.View image in fullscreenAs gaudy as it is gory … Gears 5. Photograph: MicrosoftMore importantly, Gears 5 also expands your tactical options, via an upgrade system that relates to your drone companion, Jack. Over the course of the game, you can equip Jack with a team-wide cloaking device that lets you sneak up on Locust for a silent kill, as well as an ability that lets him turn a Locust to your side. Initially these powers seem superfluous, but as the campaign’s challenge begins to bite, deploying them wisely can make the difference between victory and defeat.The tactical side of Gears 5 is even more significant in cooperative mode. Up to three players can partake in a campaign, one of whom assumes the role of Jack. Jack uses his cloaking device instead of cover for protection, floating around unseen above the battlefield until he becomes involved in the action, either deploying his abilities or zapping enemies with an electrical charge. Multiplayer has been expanded elsewhere too. Alongside the familiar Horde and Versus modes is the new Escape mode. This sees players running from a toxic green wave, battling enemies with a gradually expanding arsenal as they do so.Both thematically and tonally, Gears 5 seeks to go beyond the games that preceded it. Not only does it offer a female lead, it also calls into question the assumed heroism of the military faction the COG. Now less an army and more a nation-state, the COG has begun assimilating other settlements and cultures that have taken root in the ashes of the old world. The COG’s growingly fascist leanings are reflected in the previous game’s hero JD Fenix, whose bravery and valour gradually shift into recklessness and rage, causing concern among his friends and risking calamity for those he is supposed to protect.Given the lack of subtlety Gears titles have shown so far, the way Gears 5 seeds these ideas into a knockabout action-adventure is impressive.
