Dysarthria is a motor-speech disorder that makes it hard to control the face, mouth, and breathing muscles used for speech. Speech may sound slow, slurred, too quiet, or uneven in rhythm and pace. Symptoms range from mild to severe and can include trouble changing volume, a nasal or hoarse voice, drooling, and difficulty chewing or swallowing. Causes include stroke, brain injury, neurological diseases, infections, tongue injury, and some medicines. Diagnosis often includes a speech-language pathologist evaluation plus tests like magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), computed tomography (CT), and blood work to find the cause of your symptoms.
