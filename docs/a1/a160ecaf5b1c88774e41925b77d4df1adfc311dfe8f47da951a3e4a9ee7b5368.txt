Share Advertisement [Tyson Fury and Anthony Joshua](https://businessday.ng/sports/article/joshua-fury-cardiff-favoured-over-wembley-for-200m-heavyweight-clash/) heavyweight bout is projected to generate over N34 billion (£26 million) in ticket sales alone, putting the all-British contest among the highest-grossing boxing events in history. The rivals are scheduled to meet on December 11 at Cardiff’s Principality Stadium in a fight expected to draw significant commercial interest, with UFC chief Dana White, who says he is promoting the bout, projecting a record-breaking gate. White, in a chart with Sky Sports, described the contest as a landmark event for British boxing. “For our generation, two of the biggest, most famous heavyweights in boxing. And not even arguable that it’s the biggest UK fight ever between two British heavyweights,” he said. Read Also: [Fury vs Joshua fight sells out 80,000 tickets in 60 minutes](https://businessday.ng/sports/article/fury-vs-joshua-fight-sells-out-80000-tickets-in-60-minutes/) The projected £26 million gate would place Fury vs Joshua fourth on the list of boxing’s highest-grossing events by ticket revenue, according to the figures reported for the contest. It would also set a new benchmark for British boxing if the projection is achieved. The reported demand has underlined the commercial appeal of the long-awaited encounter. All 80,000 tickets were reportedly sold in under an hour after general sales opened, with more than 500,000 fans said to have joined the online queue. Ticket prices ranged from £88.50 for the cheapest seats to £13,128, including booking fees, for the most expensive standard tickets. Fury and Joshua dominate Britain’s biggest boxing gates The projected gate would put Fury and Joshua’s showdown at the top of the British boxing list, ahead of two other major events involving the heavyweight rivals. Anthony Joshua’s fight against Daniel Dubois reportedly generated a £14 million gate, while Tyson Fury’s contest against Dillian Whyte brought in an estimated £12 million. The projected £26 million total for Fury vs Joshua would therefore exceed both figures, reinforcing the financial significance of a contest that has taken years to arrange. However, the gate represents only one component of the event’s potential commercial value. Broadcasting rights, sponsorship agreements, and other commercial partnerships could generate additional revenue, although those figures have not been included in the ticket-sales projection.
