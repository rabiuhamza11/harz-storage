Share Advertisement Nigeria is seeking $78.5bn in [private capital to finance 65 projects across critical economic](https://businessday.ng/business-economy/article/foreign-reserves-figure-corrected-after-cbn-admits-website-glitch/) sectors as the federal government ramps up efforts to attract investors, expand productive capacity, and create jobs. Taiwo Oyedele, Finance Minister and Coordinating Minister of the Economy, alongside Olayemi Cardoso, Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN) Governor, presented the investment opportunities at the ongoing IMF-World Bank Annual Meetings in Bangkok. The projects span oil and gas, electricity, manufacturing, mining, transportation, agriculture, technology, healthcare, and urban development, according to details published on the Federal Ministry of Finance Projects Nigeria investment portal. The government estimates that the proposed investments could generate 366,763 jobs, positioning the initiative as part of its broader strategy to stimulate economic growth through private-sector financing. Oyedele stated that the government has established a dedicated Nigeria Deal Room to provide prospective investors with project information, financing requirements, and direct access to relevant government authorities. The energy sector accounts for the largest share of the investment pipeline with 25 projects requiring funding, while industry, manufacturing, and mining account for another 17. Transport and maritime infrastructure feature eight projects, agriculture and agro-processing account for six, and technology, healthcare, and urban services comprise the remaining nine. Among the proposed investments is Project Reliaxx, a crude oil evacuation and midstream infrastructure platform requiring [$1.7bn to address infrastructure bottlenecks](https://businessday.ng/business-economy/article/how-cbn-rebuilt-mpc-credibility-after-nigerias-currency-crisis/) in the oil industry where evacuation constraints impact production and revenue generation. Other key opportunities include the proposed Lagos Greenline Railway requiring $1.29bn, the Multipurpose Industrial Platforms Limited ammonia and fertiliser project with an estimated funding requirement of $2.69bn, and the $1.25bn Premium Steel and Mines integrated steel upgrade. The portfolio also features the $355.6m PELIA Flare-Gas AI Data Centre, a $105m agro-processing and cold-chain export hub in Giri, Abuja, and the New Port City development with an estimated capital requirement of $1.6bn. Oyedele noted that the government plans further reforms to tackle investment obstacles, including electricity pricing, transmission constraints, weak agricultural productivity, and regulatory inefficiencies. He identified lower inflation, reduced borrowing costs, and better coordination between fiscal and monetary policies as essential conditions for sustained economic growth. The minister also highlighted the Economic Development Incentive, which grants tax credits to eligible businesses under Nigeria’s reformed tax framework. Cardoso emphasised that reducing inflation remains vital to protecting household purchasing power, particularly for low-income Nigerians. He highlighted improvements in the capital market and banking system, noting that banks raised approximately N4.6tn during the recapitalisation exercise, with about 72% sourced from the domestic capital market. The CBN governor stated that Nigeria is targeting economic growth of 7% by 2027-2028, alongside higher oil production and increased infrastructure investment. The country plans to raise crude oil production to three million barrels per day and increase its infrastructure stock to 75% of Gross Domestic Product. Cardoso added that stronger external reserves have enhanced the nation’s capacity to withstand economic shocks. Meanwhile, the [World Bank Group stated it is helping mobilise up](https://businessday.ng/interview/article/many-developers-still-do-subscriber-financing-despite-cbn-rate-cut-olaniyan/) to $5.79bn in private financing for Nigeria in fiscal year 2026, with programs expected to support approximately 850,000 direct and indirect jobs.
